# Decisions — Pegwork

Every call and why, newest at the bottom. Format: #N date, decision, why, what it rules out.

- #1 2026-10-13: You build the machine you gamble on; duels against rivals for a pot, no rent or debt counter. Why: PLAN pillars 1 and 4; keeps clear of CloverPit and Luck Be a Landlord. Rules out slot reels, symbol grids, charms, landlords.
- #2 2026-10-13: Own fixed-step circle physics, deterministic to 1/256 px; no physics library. Why: replay, the Dry Run and pace tests need bit-identical results.
- #3 2026-10-13: The Dry Run (histogram per column) is always available for the player own board. Why: pillar 2 (the math is on the table).
- #4 2026-10-13: 384x216 logical, x5 = 1080p. Why: a board of 20x28 cells at 8 px plus a histogram fits; whole-number scaling.
- #5 2026-10-13: Art only through the pixel-art-studio skill, as build scripts in art/; Endesga 32. Why: zero budget, deterministic, one uniform hand; distinct from the Resurrect 64 games.
- #6 2026-10-13: Three trees (Tinker, Marbles, Hustle); a node must move the distribution or the knowledge of it, never a flat payout multiplier. Why: pillar 5.
- #7 2026-10-13: No real-money anything, ever.
- #8 2026-10-12: The engine layer taken from Sprocket & Sprout: loop, rng, input (Pegwork's own actions), the Web Audio shell, the jsfxr bank mechanism (empty bank), the music synth and song notation (no songs), the bitmap font, the overlap audit, the sprite atlas, the pixel helpers, the e2e harness pattern, the deploy workflow, the .bat files and Electron. Rewritten small: the IMGUI kit (no items, slots or skins) and the sheet loader (reads pixel-art-studio JSON). Not taken: S&S's songs and designed SFX (its content), anything from its sim, data, app, renderer or art. Why: CLAUDE.md's extraction list; content stays with its game.
- #9 2026-10-12: The board's 28 rows don't fit 216 px, so row 0 is the hopper above the screen (marbles release at y 4 px), row 1 is the drop rail drawn at the top edge, and the screen shows rows 1-27. No parts in rows 0-1; cups in rows 26-27. Why: DESIGN.md §1 asked M0 to decide; this costs no playfield and needs no scrolling. Rules out a scrolling board.
- #10 2026-10-12: Marbles never collide with each other. Why: each marble's path then depends only on the board and its own release, so the Dry Run of one marble is exactly what every live marble faces (pillar 2: the math on the table is the real math). Rules out marble-on-marble chaos as a mechanic.
- #11 2026-10-12: The frame has a 45° shoulder on each wall at row 3, one cell in and one cell down. Why: the lane along each wall (columns 1 and 20) can't be reached by any cell-snapped peg, and a peg in a wall column leaves a corner pocket a marble rests in; without a shoulder those columns drop straight into the x0 cups (EV 0), which fails §8.1. Rules out pegs in columns 1 and 20, and any peg within two cells of a shoulder (the tip makes a V pocket; data test).
- #12 2026-10-12: DESIGN.md §8.1 is measured at 3,000 drops a column, not 300. Why: at 300 drops a column's EV carries about ±0.06 (payout SD about 1.1) and the noisiest of 20 columns lands about ±0.12 off its true value, wider than the 0.70-0.95 band; a 300-drop pass/fail is a coin flip on the seed. The player's Dry Run stays at 300 and now shows its ± ("EV 0.82 ±0.06"), so the odds stay honest about their own noise. Pillar 2.
- #13 2026-10-12: The drop jitter is ±1 px but never within 1/16 px of dead center. Why: a jitter that rounds to exactly 0 drops a marble dead-center onto a peg and it balances there (its roll-off is below the 1/256 grid's resolution), needing a rail tap to free it: about 1 drop in 300 over a peg looked like the machine stalling. 1/16 px off center rolls off in about 11 ticks on its own. The rail tap (a seeded nudge after 0.5 s nearly still) stays as a safety net, every tap is counted, and the base board must need none (pace test).
- #14 2026-10-12: Positions round to the nearest 1/256 px; velocities round toward zero. Why: rounding can then never add speed, so no bounce gains energy even by a rounding error (physics test).
- #15 2026-10-12: The dev server for Pegwork runs on port 5183 (5173 and 5180 are taken by other sessions' servers on this PC); e2e scripts default to it, BASE= overrides.
- #16 2026-10-12: Family sprite sheets are packed by art/common.py pack() (Aseprite-compatible JSON, one frame per game sprite name, plus a pivot), not save_spritesheet. Why: save_spritesheet writes one sprite's frames; a family sheet holds several sprites with their own anchors. Same JSON shape, so any Aseprite importer still reads it.
- #17 2026-10-12: The base board's 24 pegs: a subset of a staggered lattice (even rows 4-22, a peg site every other column, alternate rows offset by one), picked by hill climb against §8.1 at 3,000 drops a column with any rail tap as a full point of penalty, checked on four seeds (best 0.94-0.95, worst 0.70-0.73, no taps in 240,000 drops). The field it found is lopsided, not symmetric: the best column is 20 and the middle columns are average. Why: a symmetric field can't put a peg under the two middle columns (cells 9 and 10 are one cell apart, which leaves a 2 px gap a marble sits in), so a symmetric board always had an open center lane straight into the x3 cup (EV 3.0 from the middle; the first lattice search found exactly that). Rules out the plinko-app look of a symmetric triangle of pegs over a symmetric cup row.
- #18 2026-10-12: Published at the owner's request: public repo ycgNem/pegwork, GitHub Pages from the deploy workflow (https://ycgnem.github.io/pegwork/). Commits, package.json author and the app id use the GitHub username ycgNem and its no-reply email, never the owner's real name. Why: the owner asked for the username.

## The pivot to Carpet Joint (2026-10-12, with the owner)

- #19 2026-10-12: The marble/plinko board is dropped; the game becomes **Carpet Joint**, a roguelike tycoon where you run a casino on a neon 1970s strip. The owner's calls, asked as options: dislikes the marble board (not the gambling, the visible math or the rivals); you are the house; an in-run Path of Exile-scale web plus a constellation-style meta tree plus prestige layers; fully active nights; design the games, floor, staff and crowd; neon 70s; start fresh in this repo (keeping the name `pegwork` and the URL). Supersedes #1, #3, #4, #6, #9–#14 and #17 (marble-board only). Still in force: #2 in spirit (own deterministic sim; no physics is needed now), #5, #7, #8, #15, #16, #18. Rules out pegs, marbles, the Dry Run, parts and the three old trees.
- #20 2026-10-12: Title **Carpet Joint** (the owner's pick from four). 1970s slang for a classy casino; you start as a "sawdust joint". The repo stays `ycgNem/pegwork`. Check itch.io and Steam before the first build carrying the name.
- #21 2026-10-12: **640×360 logical** (×3 = 1080p), 16 px floor tiles. Why: a live floor, a 300-node web and a game editor don't fit 384×216 without constant scrolling. Owner approved.
- #22 2026-10-12: Nights are live with **pause, 1× and 2×**; every order can be given while paused. The owner **oversees with a cursor**, not an avatar. Owner's calls.
- #23 2026-10-12: **The house can cheat** (gaffs), the owner's call. The cost is Heat, which brings inspectors, raids and finally a pulled license that ends the run. Pillar 2 holds: the owner always sees a gaffed table's true odds. This adds a second loss condition beside the bust. The owner chose rivals + variance as the run's pressure, so heat only builds if you cheat (or get rough with security); the *Square House* node rules gaffs out entirely.
- #24 2026-10-12: **A season is 10 nights, about 50–60 min** (the owner: "slightly shorter" than 12 nights / 60–90 min). One game hour = 27 s at 1×, so a night = 4.5 min (DESIGN §1).
- #25 2026-10-12: **Currencies:** cash (cage + bank, pillar 2), Heat as a meter (pillar 2), Know-How for the Web (pillar 5), Legend for the Marquee (pillar 5), and one currency per prestige layer for its Charter (pillar 6). **Trees:** the Web (in run) and the Marquee (between runs); Charters are lists, not trees. The owner asked for this depth.
- #26 2026-10-12: **Derivative flags**, raised in chat: the Web's shape is Path of Exile's passive tree (we use our own tier names: Angles, Specials, House Rules); the Marquee's sign sets are Grim Dawn's constellations (no affinity system; a wattage budget instead); prestige layers that add mechanics come from Antimatter Dimensions; a crowd walking a floor you laid out is the tycoon-sim convention (Theme Hospital, Two Point, Casino Inc.). Each is a reference for one quality. The Vig (the owner's other project) is a casino game played from the gambler's side; nothing is shared with it.
- #27 2026-10-12: The risk-of-ruin forecast (**the Sweat**) simulates tonight's bet flow with the live bet-resolution code instead of using the closed-form drifted-walk formula. Why: whale bets are big jumps, and the closed form assumes small steps. The formula stays as a test cross-check (DESIGN §11.7). Same principle as the old Dry Run: the math on the table is the real math.
- #28 2026-10-12: The starter games are deliberately greedy (edges of 16.7% and 8.3–12.5%, DESIGN §2), so the first lesson is editing a paytable. Pace test §11.1.
- #29 2026-10-12: The engine tooling survives the wipe (#8's list plus the art pipeline's `common.py`, `pack()` and palette lock); everything marble-specific goes in one commit. Why: the owner said to reuse nothing but the tools, and this is the tooling. Endesga 32 stays provisionally until the M0 neon test.

## Milestone 0 (2026-10-12)

- #30 2026-10-12: **Every bet settles against the cage the moment its round resolves** (a loss adds the stake, a win pays the gain; a win bigger than the cage busts the house). Why: equivalent to buy-ins and cash-outs at the cage, but it puts the bust exactly where the swing is, and the Sweat can replay it. Rules out a cage that only matters at cash-out.
- #31 2026-10-12: **Bets on one table share its outcome**, and the Sweat simulates rounds, not independent bets. Why: six tourists on one wheel pocket win together; a forecast treating them as independent would understate the swing by about √6. Rules out the closed form as the readout (#27).
- #32 2026-10-12: **The Sweat's fast path tabulates each round per outcome** (the cage change and the least cage that survives the seat order, both computed with `settle`), and a test proves it equals settling bet by bet. Why: 500 nights took 70 ms bet by bet; tabulated they take about 18 ms in Node and 19 ms in Chrome, inside the 50 ms budget, without approximating. The drum (not enumerable) settles bet by bet.
- #33 2026-10-12: **M0's Sweat holds tonight's seated crowd fixed** until 6am. Why: the arrival and departure forecast needs M1's crowd model; until then the readout says what tonight costs if the people seated now stay. Written in DESIGN §6 so nobody mistakes it for the final forecast.
- #34 2026-10-12: **Orders given while paused apply at once** (`Game.applyNow`), logged at the coming tick, refused from 6am on. Why: the owner asked for pause with every order working; the readouts (edge, Sweat) must answer before the clock moves. Replays apply the same command at the same tick, after the clock system, which does nothing before 6am, so the two orders can't differ.
- #35 2026-10-12: The wheel's two house pockets sit opposite each other (0 and 12), with colors alternating around the whole wheel (11 red, 11 black). The first layout gave 12 red and 10 black, a 0% edge on Red: the odds test caught it. The Lucky Drum is 40 balls, 10 drawn; its Pick 3 pays 2:1 for two hits and 40:1 for three (9.2%). The first draft (3:2 and 12:1) was a 50% edge.
- #36 2026-10-12: **Measured: a starter night earns +$3,004 ± $270** (20 seeds), over three times the +$900 hypothesis, and word of mouth comes out positive. DESIGN §4 records it; M1 rebalances stop-losses, the word-of-mouth penalty and the buyout prices before pace test §11.1 can pass. Not tuned silently in M0.
- #37 2026-10-12: **Art direction (M0 test, the art director's calls):** Endesga 32 stays; people 12×20 with one silhouette break per type (the tourist's sun hat); a low lamp light, slightly upper-left; tables 3/4 top-down with a crimson padded rail and gold-printed felt; neon as 2 px tubes with a white-hot edge, a magenta halo and a plum glow line, no alpha. Two tourist proportions (chibi, slim) are drawn; **the owner picks**, chibi is packed until then. Recorded in STYLE.md.
- #38 2026-10-12: **The owner picked chibi proportions** for the whole cast (head and hat ~45% of a 12×20 sprite) and **confirmed Endesga 32**. The slim variant stays in `build_tourist.py` as a reference but is never packed. The owner also approved publishing M0 and starting M1.

## Milestone 1 (2026-10-12 on)

- #39 2026-10-12: **The season is days and nights.** The sim's tick runs all season; a night is `tick - nightStart`; the day has no clock and every order applies at once. `openDoors` begins a night with the crowd the strip's split gives *as the house looks right then*, so editing paytables in the day changes tonight's crowd. The count at dawn pays wages, books the night, folds word of mouth into reputation (80% kept) and plays the rival's night and its next move. Orders given after a season's last tick replay as they did live (Game.replay).
- #40 2026-10-12: **Crowd share: U = 0.05·reputation − 14·posted edge**, the rest of the strip a fixed −0.8 until M4. Why 14 and not the M0 guess of 6: the house's take is about share × edge while edges sit under the bankroll ceiling, and share falls like e^(−k·edge), so the take peaks at 1/k; at 6 the peak was 17% (the greedy starter games were already optimal), at 14 it's about 7%. DESIGN §7.
- #41 2026-10-12: **Word of mouth −1.0 for a fast wipe-out** (down 80% within 60 minutes), and **tourist and conventioneer stop-losses at 80%**. Why: in M0 a greedy house still gained reputation (+4.4 a night) and every tourist played to zero; with these the greedy house's reputation falls to about −14 by night 4 and its share drops on 80%+ of seeds. DESIGN §5.
- #42 2026-10-12: **The Undercutter (the Silver Dollar, Lou Tolliver)** for the slice: 4.5% posted edge cutting toward 3%, about +$1,000 a night at the start, moves undercut / billboard / buffet. **Buyout = 4 × its average profit of the last 3 nights + a $7,000 building**: opens near $11,000. Measured (20 seeds): the greedy bot wins 3, the 8% tuner 8. Pace test §11.1 at slice scale. The name "Silver Dollar" is a generic 70s casino name, checked against no specific shipped game; flag if one turns up.
- #43 2026-10-12: **Staff (M1 stage B):** dealers set a table's pace and a table can't open without one; pit bosses watch up to 3 tables; security walks cheats out; the doors need a cashier. Wages are paid at dawn from the bank, then the cage, and a payroll the cage can't meet ends the season (DESIGN §3). Hiring by day from a hall of 6 rolled candidates; firing by day. Why: staff are the owner's second lever after the paytable (pillar 1, every choice moves a number on screen: pace, spot chance, wage), and the wage line makes a bigger house cost something every night. The 8% tuner bot needed one security guard to keep its 8/20 (DESIGN §11.1).
- #44 2026-10-12: **The slider re-throws a controlled roll until the bet wins (up to 6 tries)** rather than "controls one die" as the first DESIGN text said. Why: one routine that works with any bet on any dice game, through the same `compile().sample/ret` path, with a win rate the owner can compute (DESIGN §8). The past-poster doubles every win. Both raise a cheating round for the pit bosses only when they actually cheat, so a cheat on a long-shot bet is harder to spot: that's intended (they're quieter, and take less).
- #45 2026-10-12: **Accusing is the owner's call, from a gambler card** (click a gambler on the floor). A guilty accusation dispatches security like a spot; an honest one costs −2 word of mouth and the gambler leaves. A spotted cheat with no security free keeps playing, and the ticker says so. The owner's marks (a red '!' for spotted, a gold chevron for the selection) draw after everything on the floor, and under the feet of gamblers seated with their backs to us, so they never land on the felt.
