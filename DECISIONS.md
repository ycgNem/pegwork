# Decisions — Pegwork

Every call and why, newest at the bottom. Format: #N date, decision, why, what it rules out.

- #1 2026-10-13: You build the machine you gamble on; duels against rivals for a pot, no rent or debt counter. Why: PLAN pillars 1 and 4; keeps clear of CloverPit and Luck Be a Landlord. Rules out slot reels, symbol grids, charms, landlords.
- #2 2026-10-13: Own fixed-step circle physics, deterministic to 1/256 px; no physics library. Why: replay, the Dry Run and pace tests need bit-identical results.
- #3 2026-10-13: The Dry Run (histogram per column) is always available for the player own board. Why: pillar 2 (the math is on the table).
- #4 2026-10-13: 384x216 logical, x5 = 1080p. Why: a board of 20x28 cells at 8 px plus a histogram fits; whole-number scaling.
- #5 2026-10-13: Art only through the pixel-art-studio skill, as build scripts in art/; Endesga 32. Why: zero budget, deterministic, one uniform hand; distinct from the Resurrect 64 games.
- #6 2026-10-13: Three trees (Tinker, Marbles, Hustle); a node must move the distribution or the knowledge of it, never a flat payout multiplier. Why: pillar 5.
- #7 2026-10-13: No real-money anything, ever.
- #8 2026-10-12: The engine layer taken from Sprocket & Sprout: loop, rng, input (Pegwork's own actions), the Web Audio shell, the jsfxr bank mechanism (empty bank), the music synth and song notation (no songs), the bitmap font, the overlap audit, the sprite atlas, the pixel helpers, the e2e harness pattern, the deploy workflow, the .bat files and Electron. Rewritten small: the IMGUI kit (no items, slots or skins) and the sheet loader (reads pixel-art-studio JSON). Not taken: S&S's songs and designed SFX (its content), anything from its sim, data, app, renderer or art. Why: CLAUDE.md's extraction list; content stays with its game.
- #9 2026-10-12: The board's 28 rows don't fit 216 px, so row 0 is the hopper above the screen (marbles release at y 4 px), row 1 is the drop rail drawn at the top edge, and the screen shows rows 1-27. No parts in rows 0-1; cups in rows 26-27. Why: DESIGN.md §1 asked M0 to decide; this costs no playfield and needs no scrolling. Rules out a scrolling board.
- #10 2026-10-12: Marbles never collide with each other. Why: each marble's path then depends only on the board and its own release, so the Dry Run of one marble is exactly what every live marble faces (pillar 2: the math on the table is the real math). Rules out marble-on-marble chaos as a mechanic.
- #11 2026-10-12: The frame has a 45° shoulder on each wall at row 3, one cell in and one cell down. Why: the lane along each wall (columns 1 and 20) can't be reached by any cell-snapped peg, and a peg in a wall column leaves a corner pocket a marble rests in; without a shoulder those columns drop straight into the x0 cups (EV 0), which fails §8.1. Rules out pegs in columns 1 and 20, and any peg within two cells of a shoulder (the tip makes a V pocket; data test).
- #12 2026-10-12: DESIGN.md §8.1 is measured at 3,000 drops a column, not 300. Why: at 300 drops a column's EV carries about ±0.06 (payout SD about 1.1) and the noisiest of 20 columns lands about ±0.12 off its true value, wider than the 0.70-0.95 band; a 300-drop pass/fail is a coin flip on the seed. The player's Dry Run stays at 300 and now shows its ± ("EV 0.82 ±0.06"), so the odds stay honest about their own noise. Pillar 2.
- #13 2026-10-12: The drop jitter is ±1 px but never within 1/16 px of dead center. Why: a jitter that rounds to exactly 0 drops a marble dead-center onto a peg and it balances there (its roll-off is below the 1/256 grid's resolution), needing a rail tap to free it: about 1 drop in 300 over a peg looked like the machine stalling. 1/16 px off center rolls off in about 11 ticks on its own. The rail tap (a seeded nudge after 0.5 s nearly still) stays as a safety net, every tap is counted, and the base board must need none (pace test).
- #14 2026-10-12: Positions round to the nearest 1/256 px; velocities round toward zero. Why: rounding can then never add speed, so no bounce gains energy even by a rounding error (physics test).
- #15 2026-10-12: The dev server for Pegwork runs on port 5183 (5173 and 5180 are taken by other sessions' servers on this PC); e2e scripts default to it, BASE= overrides.
- #16 2026-10-12: Family sprite sheets are packed by art/common.py pack() (Aseprite-compatible JSON, one frame per game sprite name, plus a pivot), not save_spritesheet. Why: save_spritesheet writes one sprite's frames; a family sheet holds several sprites with their own anchors. Same JSON shape, so any Aseprite importer still reads it.
- #17 2026-10-12: The base board's 24 pegs: a subset of a staggered lattice (even rows 4-22, a peg site every other column, alternate rows offset by one), picked by hill climb against §8.1 at 3,000 drops a column with any rail tap as a full point of penalty, checked on four seeds (best 0.94-0.95, worst 0.70-0.73, no taps in 240,000 drops). The field it found is lopsided, not symmetric: the best column is 20 and the middle columns are average. Why: a symmetric field can't put a peg under the two middle columns (cells 9 and 10 are one cell apart, which leaves a 2 px gap a marble sits in), so a symmetric board always had an open center lane straight into the x3 cup (EV 3.0 from the middle; the first lattice search found exactly that). Rules out the plinko-app look of a symmetric triangle of pegs over a symmetric cup row.
- #18 2026-10-12: Published at the owner's request: public repo ycgNem/pegwork, GitHub Pages from the deploy workflow (https://ycgnem.github.io/pegwork/). Commits, package.json author and the app id use the GitHub username ycgNem and its no-reply email, never the owner's real name. Why: the owner asked for the username.
